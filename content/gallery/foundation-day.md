> *An evening of demos, builds and talks, all made by the students of the Astronomy Club.*

For the **20th Foundation Day**, the club filled its stall with live demonstrations, hand-built projects and short talks. Visitors could walk from a working model of a gravitational-wave detector to a homemade rocket in a few steps.

---

##  The LIGO Demo

The headline exhibit. LIGO detects ripples in spacetime by measuring changes far smaller than an atom, and our demo built that idea up one piece at a time.

**1. Gravitational waves.** We started with the basics: what they are, what produces them, and why detecting them was such a milestone.

**2. The Michelson interferometer.** Light is split along two paths and recombined. A tiny change in either path length shows up as a change in the pattern. This is the core of LIGO.

**3. The Fabry-Perot cavity.** Mirrors make the light travel back and forth many times, which stretches the effective path and boosts sensitivity.

**4. Suspension models.** Hanging the mirrors so they are isolated from vibration is what lets such faint signals be measured at all.
