Two years ago, I owned three cameras, five lenses, two tripods, and a pile of accessories. Every photo walk felt like moving house. Yet the days that produced the strongest images were often the ones when I carried only one camera.

## The Process of Letting Go

I began simplifying my gear after a trip. I had packed two camera bodies and three lenses, but used only a 35mm prime the entire time. When I came home, I listed the other lenses for sale.

The moment I sold them, I felt unexpectedly lighter. The things I thought I "might use someday" had never really been used. They only occupied my bag, my attention, and my bank account.

## My Current Kit

After two years of simplifying, my photography kit is down to this:

**Camera bodies**
- One full-frame mirrorless camera for daily use
- One film camera for occasional experiments

**Lenses**
- 35mm f/1.4, my everyday lens for about 90 percent of my photos
- 85mm f/1.8 for portraits and close details

**Accessories**
- A light carbon-fiber tripod
- One spare battery
- A 128 GB SD card
- A small camera bag that fits everything

That is all. Together, it weighs less than three kilograms and fits easily into a small bag.

## Why 35mm

People often ask why I kept the 35mm lens. My answer is simple: it feels closest to the way I see.

With a 35mm lens, you do not zoom. You move your feet. Step forward and the frame tightens. Step back and it opens up. That process forces you to think about composition, observe the environment, and build a relationship with your subject.

> Limits are not restraints; they are catalysts for creativity.

When you have only one focal length, you observe and compose more carefully. You stop hesitating over whether another lens might be better and focus on the image in front of you.

## Less Is More

After simplifying my gear, my photography improved.

When I used to carry several lenses, I spent too much time deciding which one to use and missed many good moments. Now I pick up the camera and shoot, which keeps my attention on the frame itself.

More importantly, I go out to shoot more often. Because the kit is light, I can carry it anytime. Because I carry it anytime, I miss fewer good scenes.

In photography, the mind behind the lens matters far more than the lens itself. Gear is only a tool. What truly decides whether a photo works is your taste, your observation, and the moment you press the shutter.

If you are struggling over which lens to buy, first ask yourself whether you have truly reached the limits of the lens already in your hands.

The answer may save you a lot of money.
