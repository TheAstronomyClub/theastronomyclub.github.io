In June, London sunlight can feel unusually generous. I stood on the Millennium Bridge and looked toward the dome of St Paul's Cathedral, solemn and bright beneath the blue sky.

## Arrival

As I crossed the bridge, the wind tangled my hair. Tourists filled the walkway: some holding selfie sticks, others leaning against the railing to look over the Thames. I walked slowly, wanting to hold the scene a little longer.

The cathedral's pale stone facade glowed warmly in the sun. I climbed the steps and pushed open the heavy wooden doors. Inside, the light dropped suddenly, and my eyes needed a few seconds to adjust.

> Architecture is frozen music, and a cathedral is one of its most solemn movements.

## Beneath the Dome

Entering the nave, my first impression was height. The dome seemed almost endless, rising beyond the reach of my sight. Sunlight entered through high windows and formed columns of light on the floor, with dust drifting slowly inside them.

I found a bench and sat down, doing nothing but watching the light move. Around me, some people prayed quietly and others spoke in low voices. Footsteps expanded in the open space, then were quickly absorbed.

- The paintings on the dome told biblical stories
- The organ sounded occasionally from somewhere far away
- Light and shadow on the stone floor changed every minute

## Climbing Up

After hesitating for a while, I decided to climb up into the dome. The stairway was narrow, wide enough for only one person, and spiraled upward in a way that made me dizzy.

When I reached the viewing platform, the wind was strong, but the view was wide enough to make me forget the climb. London spread out below: the Thames like a silver ribbon, the Shard shining in the distance, and red double-decker buses moving through the streets like tiny toys.

## Leaving

By the time I walked back down, evening had arrived. The setting sun turned the stone golden and stretched the shadows long. I looked back one last time. In the dusk, the dome seemed even more still.

Some places feel like simple tourist spots before you visit them. After you leave, they return unexpectedly in small moments and make you smile. St Paul's Cathedral is that kind of place for me.
